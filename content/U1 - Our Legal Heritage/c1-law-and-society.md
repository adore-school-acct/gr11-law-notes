# C1 - Law and Society 

## The Law and Law vs. Rules

**Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms:** section of the Constituion Act (1982) that protects our rights and freedoms at the highest level of gov't.

**rule:** a condition / task that is imposed on you

**law:** a rule that is imposed on everyone in a juridisction which everyone has to follow

## Impact of Law

Law regulates our activities from birth to death

- restrictions on marriage and divorce
- who gets our money when we die
- what products we can purchase
- how fast can we drive
- and more...

*Law* is a civilized people's attempt to regulate life by reason and fairness as opposed to force

## Rule of Law

**Rule of Law:** a three-part principle of justice related to law:

1. Individuals must recognize and accept that **law is necessary** to regulate society
2. The law **applies equally** to everyone, including to people of power
3. No one has the authority to **take away our rights** except by following the law

## Law, Morality, and Justice

### Moral Questions

- Should it be illegal to help someone die suffering from an illness?
- Is the death penalty just?
- Should abortion be criminalized?
- Should parents physically discipline their children?
- Why are tobacco and alcohol legal but not other drugs?

**Laws** reflect our collective community standards and beliefs

#### Equality

"Unjust" equality: A person who breaks in to save a drowning friend and a person who steals money from others are treated the same

"Just" equality: Everyone, regardless of race, gender, religion, etc. are treated equally

### Characteristics of Justice

- **Treat like cases alike and different cases differently**
  - i.e. a person is suffering from Tourette's Syndrome (TS), characterized by uncontrollable noises
  - a disturbance case against him should be treated differently than a normal person
- **No discrimination based on irrelevant characteristics**
  - Just: A young child is not allowed to drink alcohol
  - Unjust: A person with dark skin is not allowed to drink alcohol
- **Justice is impartial; laws applied regardless of person's position or status**
  - Celebrities who murder a friend should still be charged with murder
- **Conforms to society's values and beliefs**
  - Law requiring DNA samples to be taken at birth could be violation of privacy
  - Depends on whether the public values protection over privacy

## Historical Roots of Law

### The *Code of Hammurabi* (1792-1750 BCE)

During Hammurabi's reign over Babylon (located in present-day Iraq), he **codified** the rules and penalties of Babylonian life

**codified:** arranged and recorded systematically

*Sample Laws*

- If anyone is committing a robbery and is caught, then he shall be put to death. But if the robber is not caught then shall he who was robbed claim under oath the amount of his loss, [and] then shall the community... compensate him for the goods stolen.
- If anyone opens his ditches to water his crops, but is careless, and the water floods the fields of his neighbour, then he shall pay for the loss.
- If a man takes a wife and she is seized by disease, and if he then desires to take a second wife, he shall not put away his [first] wife.  But he shall keep her in the house and support her as long as she lives.
- If a physician operates and kills the patient, the physician's hands shall be cut off.
- If a man strikes a woman and she dies, the man's daughter shall be put to death.
- If a slave says to his master, 'You are not my master,' his master can cut off his ear.
- If anyone brings an accusation of any crime before the elders and does not prove what he has charged, he shall, if it be a capital offense charged, be put to death.
- If a judge shall try a case, reach a decision, and present his judgement in writing, and if later, error shall appear in his decision and it be through his own fault, then he shall be publicly removed from the judge's bench, and never again shall he sit there to render judgement.
- If anyone steals the property of a temple or a court, he shall be put to death, and also the one who receives the stolen thing from him shall be put to death.
- If anyone steals cattle or sheep, a pig or a goat, the thief shall pay tenfold; if the thief has nothing with which to pay he shall be put to death.
- [Not translated] People should not lie, especially at trial
- [N.T.] The strong should protect the weak

***Babylonian Hiearchy of Power***

1. Gods
2. King
3. Male Nobles
4. Wives and children of these nobles
5. Poor
6. Slaves

#### Basis of Hammurabi's Laws

**retribution:** "eye for an eye" justice, based on vengenance and punishment

> no distinction made between an accident and deliberate action

**restitution:** payment made by offender to the victim of a crime

### Mosaic Law

**Mosaic Law:** laws given to Moses to guide the Hebrew people, and recorded in Bible

***Ten Commandments:*** the first ten laws at the beginning of Mosaic Law

Jewish people call their religious books the *Torah* (the law)

Forbidden to commit murder, adultery, theft, and worship other gods

#### Laws from the Bible

- Thou shalt not kill.
- Thou shalt not steal.
- If a man steals an ox or a sheep and kills it or sells it, he shall restore five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep.
- If a thief is found breaking in and be smitten that he die, there shall be no blood shed for him.
- Thous shalt not raise a false report, or put thine hand with the wicked to be an unrighteous witness.

Mosiac law focused on punishing a deliberate action

### Greek Law

First form of democracy born in ancient Greece

Only small % of Athenian people (citizens), could vote

Women, children, aliens, and slaves were not citizens

*Citizen expectations and duties:* Vote and jury duty

Large #s of people became judges (peak 6000 judges) &rarr; later limited to 101, 501, or 1001 citizens

Greeks used democratic means to sentence an accused

**Trial of Socrates (399 BCE)**

- Accusation: misled the youth and undermined the democracy
- 281 voted guilty : 220 voted innocent
- Accusers wanted death penalty
- Socrates argued that law he was charged under is unfair but he has been tried fairly
- Drank poisonous hemlock as punishment

### Roman Law

**Principles**

1. law must be recorded
2. justice could not be left to judges alone

**Twelve Tables:** earliest roman codes written on wood and bronze in 450 BCE

*Example Laws*

- Sons shall be under the jurisdiction [legal authority] of the father.
- Women aren't mentioned since they are not considered "persons."

100 CE: Romans had complex society and came complex laws, *legal advisers* would advise Roman people (precursor to lawyers)

### *Justinian's Code*

Roman law became too complex

**Justinian's Code (529 CE):** a new body of law created by emperor Justinian I (527-565 CE) to clarify the law

The word *justice* dervied from Justinian

## Roots of British and Canadian Law

### Early British Law

When Romans left Britain around 410 CE, they turned to God to determine guilt or innocence.

**trial by ordeal:** requires the accused to undergo torture to determine guilt or innocence

- "trial by hot iron": accused holds hot piece of iron and then bandaged to heal; if wound healed when bandage removed, person is innocent
  - wound rarely healed because bandages were not sterile
- "trial by hot water"
- "trial by cold water" (called "swimming a witch" for witchcraft cases)

**trial by oath helping:** requires people who knew the accused to swear on the Bible that he/she was innocent; *most people feared God's wrath*

**trial by combat:** two parties involved in a dispute engaged in a duel &mdash; God would save the innocent

#### Adversarial System

**adversarial system:** both sides, represented by lawyers, battle in "the court" in front of a judge to determine guilt or innocence

### The Feudal System

William the Conqueror, Duke of Normandy (northern Frnce) invaded and conquered England at the Battle of Hastings

He set out to ensure his word was law

**divine right:** belief that monarchs got their power from God, they are "above the law" and accountable only to God

- Britian divided into parcels of land
- Each parcel controlled by a nobleman
- Everything within the parcel (land, animals, and the peasants farming land) were its nobleman's property
- Some noblemen were fair and just but others were cruel and harsh
- Punishments for similar offences varied
- 12th century: People rose up and complained to then king Henry II

### Common Law

Henry II authorized judges to travel to various villages and resolve disputes

*Factors of this new system*

- **assize:** travelling courts
- **circuit judge:** judges of assizes
- **case law:** deciding cases based on recorded decisions of similar cases
- **common law:** law developed in English courts, relies on case law, common to all people
- ***stare decisis:*** "to stand by the decision" (Latin)
- **rule of precedent:** applying a prev. decision to a case w/ similar circumstances

### Legal Reforms and the *Magna Carta*

**Magna Carta:** charter of political and civil rights

- reluctantly signed by King John in June 1215
- establishes the *Rule of Law*
- no one, not even the king is above the law

***habeas corpus:*** "you must have the body" &mdash; court order that says anyone detained must appear to court within a reasonable amount of time

> Designed to prevent unlawful arrest

### Aboriginal Law

- 1450: The 5 nations of the Mohawk, Onondaga, Seneca, Oneida, and Cayuga came together to form the *Iroquois / Haudenosaunee Confederacy*
- 1720: Tuscarora joins Iroquois Confederacy (Six Nations)
- **Great Binding Law:** constitution of the Iroquois Confederacy
- above law outlined rights, duties, and responsibilities of the people

*Excerpt: **On Clans and Consanguinity***

44. The lineal descent of the people...shall run in the female line. Women shall be considered the progenitors of the Nation. They shall own the land and the soil. Men and women shall follow the status of the mother.
45. The women heirs of the Confederated Lordship titles ahll be called Royaneh [or Nobles] for all time to come.

...

47. If the female heirs of a Confederate Lord's title become extinct, the title right shall be given by the Lords of the Confederacy to the sister family whom they shall elect and that family shall hold the name and transmit it to their [female] heirs, but they shall not appoint any of their sons as a candidate for a title until all the eligible men of the former family shall have died or otherwise have become ineligible.

*simplify:* women of the family are given power, and women of related families are given power; men are only given power as a last resort
