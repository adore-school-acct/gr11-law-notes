# C11 - Sentencing and Correctional System

## Goals of Sentencing

**sentence:** punishment imposed on a person convicted of committing a crime

### Protection of the Public

- Main goal of sentencing
- Includes protection of public's
	- person
	- property
	- individual rights and freedoms
- Person harms everyone in society when someone commits offence
- People feel threatened until offender is apprehended and public protection restored

### Retribution

- **retribution:** punishment to avenge a crime, ensuring that offenders suffer consequences of their actions
- "eye for an eye"
- society often wants an offender to "pay" for their crimes

### Deterrence

- **specific deterrence:** punishment to discourage criminals from reoffending
- **general deterrence:** punishment to discourage people in general from offending
- many believe punishment "sends a message"

### Rehabilitation

- **rehabilitation:** punishment combined with treatment and training to help offenders function in society
- Offers services and programs like...
	- psychiatric and medical treatment for drug / alcohol abuse
	- educational programs to prepare offenders to reintegrate with society
	- i.e. prison job workshop to help a jobless person who stole to find a job
- **recidivism:** returning to crime after release from prison
- Rehab programs matching w/ offender's needs reduce recidivism rates by 50%

### Restitution

- **restitution:** punishment that requires offender to pay the victim / society back for the harm or loss suffered
- i.e. community service, payment for damages

### Denunciation

**denunciation:** punishment designed to send a message that offender's conduct has violated society's basic values and that such conduct will be punished

## Sentencing Procedures

### Perspectives to Consider

#### Considering the Offender

- Judge may order...
- **pre-sentence report:** background information about convicted offender, prepared for Judge prior to sentencing
	- incl. info. about background,
		- family
		- education
		- employment history
		- physical / mental health
		- social activities
		- potential ability
		- motivation
		- friends
	- must be objective to provide "a picture of the accused as a person in society"
- **psychiatric assessment:** report describing mental history of offender

#### Considering the Victim

- **victim impact statement:** statement prepared by victim or their family describing harm done or loss suffered as a result of offence
- s. 722 of *Criminal Code* requires Court to consider any statement prepared by victim
- flexible presentation to Court, as long as it's appropriate

#### Considering Society

- Crown has right to recommend appropriate sentence if accused found guilty
- i.e. Crown introduces prev. criminal record to argue for substantial prison term
- Crown's role to ensure society's interests are protected during sentencing

### The Sentencing Hearing

- **sentencing hearing:** the Judge's opportunity to listen to recommendations and to consider all the facts before passing sentence
- Both defence counsel and Crown present recommendations to Judge
- Judge must examine:
	- criminal record of the accused
	- pre-sentence report findings
	- nature and severity of the crime
	- offender's background
	- circumstances leading to and surrounding the offence
	- offender's family and employment situation
	- offender's attitude toward his/her own conduct
- Judge sentencing restricted by...
	- max. and min. limits specified in statute laws
	- precedents for similar crimes
	- aggravating and mitigating factors
	- **aggravating factors:** circumstances that may increase the severity of the sentence
		- i.e. ongoing pattern of assault against same person
	- **mitigating factors:** circumstances that may decrease the severity of the sentence
		- i.e. first time offence and evidence of remorse

### Aggravating and Mitigating Factors

#### Concerning the Offender

|Aggravating Factors|Mitigating Factors|
|-|-|
|premeditation|impulsive act|
|previous criminal record|young or first-time offender|
|large profits from the offence|cooperating with police|
|ring leader of the group|mental or physical disability
|continuing offence over time|short life expectancy|

#### Concerning the Offence

|Aggravating Factors|Mitigating Factors|
|-|-|
|violent offence|minor offence|
|number of victims|time spent in custody|
|need for deterrent|delay in trial|

## Types of Traditional Sentences

### Discharges

- most lenient sentence
- **absolute discharge:** releasing a convicted offender and erasing criminal record after one year
- **conditional discharge:** releasing a convicted offender under certain terms, and erasing the criminal record after 3 yrs. if terms are met
	- term ex: avoiding contact, observing curfew
	- violation: discharge revoked, more severe sentence imposed

### Probation

- **probation:** sentence that allows a person to live in the community under supervision of parole officer
	- comes from latin word *probatio* (means "proof")
- allows convicted offender to prove he/she is able to live in community w/o committing another offence
- offender must comply w/ specific set of conditions
- failure to comply = additional terms -or- revokal of probation order w/ original sentencing
- *3 Compulsory Conditions*
	- keep the peace and demonstrate good behaviour
	- appear in court when required
	- notify the Court or probation officer of any change of name, address, or employment
- *Optional Conditions*
	- reporting regularly to probation officer
	- not associating with known criminals
	- staying away from particular locations
	- attending counselling sessions
	- paying for damages resulting from the offence
	- refraining from alcohol and illegal drug use

### Suspended Sentence

- **suspended sentence:** judgement but not carried out, provided certain requirements are met
- can only be imposed for offences w/ no min. punishment
- usually given for
	- first-timers
	- minor offences
	- situations where the Judge feels that knowledge of sentence alone is sufficient to keep offender in line
- offender still has criminal record
- could be placed on probation

### Intermittent Sentence

- **intermittent sentence:** prison sentence of <90 days that can be served on weekends and at night
- offender can serve time in intervals instead of all at once
- usually given to offenders who are...
	- not violent
	- have steady jobs
	- whose families depend on their income
- Court always indicates when offender must report to jail
- Offender subject to probation order when not in jail

### Conditional Sentence

- **conditional sentence:** prison term of <2 yrs. that can be served in the community, w/ strict terms attached
- can be imposed if offence has no min. sentence
- stricter terms than for probation / suspended sentence
	- i.e. performing community service
	- seeking treatment for drug addiction

### Electronic Monitoring (EM)

- **electronic monitoring (EM):** allowing an offender to serve a sentence at home under electronic supervision from remote location
- came from increasing prison costs
- exceptions allowed for some offenders who
	- have jobs outside the home
	- wish to attend devotional services
- offender wears electronic bracelet that signals alarm when he/she leaves home base
- other systems
	- random telephone dialing to confirm offender on premises
	- video camera near telephone
	- breathing testing remotely

### Binding Over, Deportation, and Fines

- **binding over:** sentence ordering the defendant to keep the peace and demonstrate good behaviour for up to 12 months
	- commonly known as "peace bond"
	- may be imposed on someone who threatens another person or person's family / property
	- not a sentence because no crime has been committed
- **restraining order:** order issued immediately to stop someone from taking harmful action
- **deportation:** expelling an offender from the country
	- applies to anyone who is not Canadian citizen
- **fines:** specific amounts of money paid as penalties for offences
	- max. fine of $2,000 for summary conviction offences
	- optional for indictable offences with min. prison sentence and max. prison term <5 yrs.

### Suspension of Privileges

**suspension of privilege:** sentence that withholds a privilege for a specified period or a lifetime

i.e. impaired driving

### Plea Bargaining

- **plea bargain:** negotiated deal whereby accused pleads guilty in exchange for lighter sentence
- Majority of people charged w/ criminal offences never go to trial
- In return for lighter sentence, accused exchanges information or pleads guilty to lesser charge
- Once bargain struck, it is presented to Judge, who usually accepts

### Incarceration

- **incarceration:** imprisonment for a *specified length of time*
- each criminal offence has max. sentence
- max. sentences rarely imposed due to severity
- Judges use range of punishments in determining appropriate sentences

#### Length of Imprisonment

- Depends on several factors like penalty in *Criminal Code*, details about crime
	- and offender, and whether multiple sentences should be served
- <2 year sentence: provincial facilties
- &ge;2 yrs: federal facilities
- **dangerous offender:** someone who constitutes a threat to life, safety, or phyiscal and mental well-being of other persons *(rare cases)*
	- in such cases, s. 753 (4) of *Code* requires Judge to impose **indeterminate sentence**
	- **indeterminate sentence:** a prison sentence for an indefinite period
	- offender may apply for parole but will remain in prison until parole board believes he/she is safe for the community
- **concurrent sentences:** sentences served at the same time
	- i.e. 2 sentences of 4 yrs. = 4 yrs.
- **consecutive sentences:** sentences served one after another
	- i.e. 2 sentences of 4 yrs. = 8 yrs.
	- rare in Canada

## Restorative Justice Programs

**restorative justice (a.k.a. alternative justice):** justice that focuses on helping to heal the offender and reintegrate them to the community

### Comparison and Goals

**Goals:**

- restore / rebuild offender's self-respect
- empower the offender and the victim to address their conflict
- facilitate reconciliation between offender and victim within community
- s. 717 of *Code* only allows restorative justice in certain situations
	- they must not interfere with protection of society

|In the Courts|In Restorative Justice Programs|
|-|-|
|Crime defined as a violation of rules and as harm to the state|Crime seen as harm done to victims and communities|
|Victim has limited opportunity to speak about their injury, loss, and needs during trial|Victim is central to the process of defining harm and how it might be repaired|
|Offender, victim, and community remain passive and have little responsibility for a resolution|Offender, victim, and community actively participate in resolution resulting from restorative forum
|Community's role is limited|Community is actively involved in holding offenders accountable, supporting victims, and providing opportunities for offenders to make amends|
|Restitution is rare|Restitution is normal|
|Process is controlled and operated by the state and professionals who may seem remote|Process is overseen by the state, but is usually driven by communties|
|Offender is blamed, stigmatized, and punished|Offender is reintegrated into the community, and his/her dignity is preserved|
|Repentance and forgiveness are rarely considered|Repentance and forgiveness are encouraged|
|Process assumes win-loss outcomes|Process makes win-win outcomes possible

### Victim-Offender Mediation

- **victim-offender mediation:** an alternative measures program designed to determine restitution involving victim, offender, and mediator
- Four Stages
	- case referral and acceptance
	- preparation
	- the mediation itself
	- follow-up
- First stage often takes place after conviction / formal admission of guilt
- Occasionally, referral occurs prior to conviction in attempts to avoid prosecution altogether
- Form of mediation win-win
	- decreases incidence of criminal behaviour
	- reduces feelings of fear and anxiety among victims
- Offender and victim meet with trained mediator
- Both parties present their version of events and express feelings regarding offence
- Participants agree to appropriate restitution; may incl. payment and/or services

### Family Group Conferencing

- **family group conferencing:** alternative measures program in which victim and offender meet with family members and other concerned parties to determine restitution
	- may also include
		- community support groups
		- police officers
		- social welfare officers
		- lawyers
	- only used when offender admits guilt
- Official reminds offender that he/she has right to end conference at any time and proceed to court instead before conf. begins
- Trained coordinator monitors process to ensure offender knows that it's his/her *conduct* that is being denounced, not his *person*
- 3 Stages
	- preparation
	- conference itself
	- post-conference
- Order of Conference Events
	- Offender begins conference w/ explanation of events
	- Victim explains events
	- Questioning of one another and by family members
	- Restitution discussed privately by offender and his/her family before presented as offer
	- Negotiations continue until consensus reached
	- Coordinator monitors terms of agreement for successful completion
	- Agreement violated: case returns to court

### Victim-Offender Panels

- **victim-offender panels:** moderated discussions that allow victims and offenders, linked by a common crime (not *same* crime), to express feelings about offence
- happens when victims and offenders don't want to / cannot meet each other
- **victim impact panel:** panel allowing victims and drunk driving offenders to express their views and feelings
	- organized by Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD)

### Aboriginal Sentencing Circles

- **sentencing circle:** alternative measures program that involves a process of healing for both the victim and the offender
- for Aboriginal offenders &rarr; sentence &le; 2 yrs. prison
- comprised of
	- offender
	- offender's family
	- victim
	- victim's family
	- elders
	- other members of offender's Aboriginal community
	- police officers
	- trial judge
- Process involves separating the *person* from the *criminal act* committed
- All participants in circle do their best to convey to offender that there are people who care for his / her well-being
- Balance and consensus important traditional values
- Everyone participates by offering an opinion
- Requires patience, thought, deliberation, and speaking skill

## The Correctional System

- Toughest and most expensive penalty for offenders in Canada
- Costs $3 bil. annually
- Cost of keeping offender in federal prison: $95k / yr.
	- cost for community supervision for parolees: $2k - $9.5k / yr.
- 120 prisoners / 100,000 people in Canada (< 1/4 of US)

### The Provincial Correctional System

|Closed custody|Protective custody|Open custody|
|-|-|-|
|secured facility|separation from others in prison|less secure facility (i.e. halfway house)|
|dangerous offenders or flight risks|prisoners requiring psychological care or protection from other prisoners|non-violent offenders who aren't flight risks|

### The Federal Correctional System

Prisons called institutions

|Maximum-security|Medium-security|Minimum-security|
|-|-|-|
|secured with high walls, razor-wire fences, and bars on all doors / windows|fewer physical barriers|no external physical barriers|
|armed correctional officers|fewer correctional officers|unarmed correctional officers|
|dangerous offenders|prisoners have more freedom and more contact with each other|employment and educational programs to assist in transition back to society|

#### Correctional Programs

- Correctional Service of Canada (CSC) responsible for care and well-being of inmates in fed. jails
- Helps provide food, clothing, housing, health care
- Helps people deal w/ issues relating to their criminal behaviour
- Every prisoner has correctional plan that outlines his / her needs and priorities
- All inmates required to attend school / work
- CSC offers programs in following areas:
	- life skills
	- cognitive skills training
	- substance abuse intervention
	- sex offender treatment
	- family violence intervention
	- literacy
	- work experience

## Paroles and Pardons

- **parole:** release of an inmate, on a promise of good behaviour, into the community before full sentence is served
- prisoners must be reviewed for parole after 1/3 of full sentence or 7 yrs., whichever is first
	- exception: first-degree murder
- governed by *Corrections and Conditional Release Act*
- review does not always result in parole
- inmates must meet certain conditions to qualify for parole

### Parole Decisions

- Decided by the National Parole Board (NPB) w/ regional offices around country
- Protection of society most important factor
- Parole granted only if members believe offender will not pose risk to society...
- ... and will return to community as law-abiding citizen
- Board members review these in deciding parole
	- the offence
	- criminal history
	- social problems, such as drug use or family violence
	- mental status, especially if it affects likelihood of future crime
	- performance on earlier releases, if any
	- relationships and employment opportunities
	- psychological or psychiatric reports
	- opinions from professionals, such as police officers and social workers
	- victim impact statement
- After board completes risk assessments, it looks at specific info. about inmate, like
	- behaviour while incarcerated
	- evidence that behaviour has changed
	- benefit from correctional programs
	- treatment for any disorder diagnosed by a professional

### Conditional Release

- **conditional release:** serving part of the sentence in the community under supervision
- **unescorted temporary absence:** brief release from custody for community service or for personal reasons
	- personal reasons, ex: medical or administrative issues, family contact
- **work release:** conditional absence from custody to perform paid or voluntary work under supervision
- **day parole:** conditional absence from custody during the day only
	- for low-risk offenders
- **full parole:** conditional release from custody after serving 1/3 to 1/2 of a sentence
- **statutory release:** release, by law, after serving 2/3 of a sentence

#### Eligibility for...

| |Unescorted Temporary Absence|Day Parole|Full Parole|Statutory Release|

|**Low-risk offenders**|Whichever shorter: 1/6 of sentence or 3 yrs. (NPB or CSC decision)|Accelerated Review Process, after 1/6 of sentence (NPB decision)|After 1/3 to 1/2 of sentence (NPB decision)|Automatic after 2/3 sentence|

#### Conditions for Release

Offender must agree to *all* of following conditions for release. Offender must:

- travel directly on release to place of residence indicated on release certificate
- report to a parole supervisor immediately
- continue to report to parole supervisor as instructed
- provide the parole supervisor with an address for place of residence
- immediately report any changes in...
	- address
	- occupation
	- educational training
	- volunteer work
	- family
	- domestic or financial situation
	- any other change that might reasonably affect their ability to keep conditions of parole
- remain in Canada at all times and within established boundaries
- obey the law and keep the peace
- inform the parole supervisor if arrested or questioned by police
- always carry release certificate and identity card provided by releasing authority and be ready to produce them on request
- report to the police as instructed by parole supervisor
- not own, possess, or have any control over any weapon as defined in the *Criminal Code* except as allowed by the parole supervisor
- if on day parole, to return to the penitentiary at the date and time set out on release certificate

### Pardon

- **pardon:** the setting aside of a person's record of conviction
- Any federal agency with conviction records must keep those records separate
- Information may not be disclosed w/o permission from Solicitor General of Canada
- Pardon *does not* erase fact that person was convicted of offence
- Offender convicted of summary offence can apply for pardon *3 yrs. after completing sentence*
- Indictable offence &rarr; pardon after 5 years
- Offender must demonstrate that he / she is now a law-abiding citizen
- Pardon automatically revoked if person later convicted of an indictable offence
- NPB can rescind pardon for any of the following:
	- conviction for summary offence
	- unacceptable conduct
	- false information at time of application
- **Royal Prerogative of Mercy:** release or sentence reduction granted by the Queen
	- takes form of clemency (release) or sentence commuted (reduced)
