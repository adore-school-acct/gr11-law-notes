# C7 - Investigation and Arrest

## Levels of Police (Canada)

### Federal Police (RCMP)

- RCMP founded in 1873 as North West Mounted Police
- RCMP: Royal Canadian Mounted Police
- Provide investigative and protective services to the federal gov't.
- Serve as provincial police for all provinces and territories excl.
	- Ontario
	- Québec
	- Newfoundland and Labrador
- Serve as municipal police in some communities
- Only police force in Nunavut, Yukon, and N.W.T.

#### Federal Policing Mandate

1. Border Integrity
	- Customs and Excise Program
	- Immigration and Passport Branch
	- Integrated Border Enforcement Teams (IBETs)
	- Federal Enforcement Program
	- Marine and Ports Branch
2. Drugs and Organized Crime
	- Drug Enforcement Branch
	- Organized Crime Branch
	- Drugs and Organized Crime Awareness Service
3. International Policing
	- International Operations Branch
	- International Peace Operations Branch
	- INTERPOL Ottawa
	- International Travel and Visits Branch
4. Financial Crime
	- Commercial Crime Branch
	- Proceeds of Crime Program
	- Integrated Market Enforcement Team (IMETs)

### Provincial Police

- Jurisdiction in rural areas and in unincorporated regions around cities
- Provincial police forces (by size)
	1. Ontario Provincial Police (OPP)
	2. Sureté du Québec
	3. Royal Newfoundland Constabulary

#### Responsibities (from Ontario's *Police Services Act*)

- policing municipalities that are not required by law to maintain their own police force
- responding to municipal police requests for special assistance in emergencies
- providing traffic control on all 400-series and major highways, incl. sections under municipal police force jurisdiction
- providing investigative services, on request, to the coroner's office and to other provincial ministries
- performing other assigned duties, like
	- maintaining the provincial firearms registry
	- providing security at Queen's Park
	- protecting Ontario government officials and dignitaries

### Municipal Police

- jurisdiction over policing in their respective town and city in Canada
- funded by their own municipality
- usually organized in numbered divisions that serve local community
	- divisions divided into squads
	- **squads** specialize in certain types of crimes
- Examples of squads / units
	- Gang Crime Unit
	- Robbery Squad
	- Homicide Squad
	- Explosives Disposal Unit

#### Duties of Officer

- preserving peace
- preventing crimes
- assisting crime victims
- apprehending criminals
- laying charges and participating in prosecutions
- executing warrants
- enforcing municipal bylaws

### Aboriginal Police

- First Nations Policing Policy administered by Department of the Solicitor General

## Starting a Police Investigation

### Arriving at the Crime Scene

**crime scene:** location / site where criminal offence took place

Tasks officers perform (in order)

1. Call ambulance and assist injured ppl. at scene
2. Call in reinforcements to help eliminate remaining hazards
	1. i.e. fires, unexploded bombs
3. Search crime scene even if witnesses say suspects have left

### Protecting and Preserving the Crime Scene

- Officers must establish 2 boundaries: centre and perimeter
- **centre:** area where offence was actually committed
- **perimeter:** surrounding area of centre, where offender may have been present or left evidence
- *Reason for Preserving Crime Scenes*
	- to allow for thorough search of scene
	- to seize and collect physical evidence
	- to ensure physical evidence seized is admissible in court
- **contamination:** loss, destruction, or alteration of physical evidence
	- happens when evidence not managed properly
	- contaminated evidence may not be admissible in court
	- leads police to draw inaccurate conclusions
- **police log:** written record of what an officer has witnessed
	- documents daily activities
	- help officers recall events, when at trial

### Officers' Roles at Crime Scene

- 4 types of cops
- **"patrol officer":** officer responsible for securing crime scene and ensuring evidence is not lost or tampered with
	- has "beat" / area to check regularly
	- first member typically at scene
	- usually wraps yellow tape around perimeter
	- may arrest suspects if they come upon crime in progress
- **"scenes of crime officer":** officer trained in evidence collection and preservation techniques
	- usually skilled photographers
	- trained in lifting fingerprints, foot/tire impressions
	- also collect blood and hair evidence
	- tend to work on less serious offences like break and enter, car thefts
- **"criminal identification officer":** officer responsible for searching, gathering, and analyzing evidence at scene &mdash; responsible for...
	- searching crime scene
	- examining scene for physical evidence
	- gathering and analyzing evidence
	- sending certain types of evidences to a lab for analysis
- **"criminal investigations bureau officer":** plainclothes dective with experience in a particular area of crime
	- such as homicide, robbery, or sexual offences
	- trained to...
		- supervise investigaion
		- interview victims and witnesses
		- interrogate suspects
		- draw conclusions from physical evidence
		- arrest suspects

## Identifying and Collecting Physical Evidence

- Crown must prove beyond reasonable doubt that accused was present at crime scene
- **physical evidence:** any object, impression, or body element that can be used to prove/disprove facts relating to an offence
	- carries greater weight in court that evidence from witness statements
- **forensic science:** application of biochemical and other scientific techniques to analyze evidence in a criminal investigation
	- i.e. forensic scientists
	- pathologists (does autopsies, how/when person has died)
	- entomologists (insect specialist) &mdash; life stages of insects in body (time of death)

### Tools

- Tools used in commission of crimes
- i.e. hammers, knives, screwdriver, crowbars
- may have individual characteristics (i.e. surfaces) detectable in crime labs

### Impressions

- **impressions:** patterns or marks found on surfaces caused by various objects, such as...
	- fingers
	- gloves
	- shoes
	- tires
	- tools
- Stages of Recording
	1. Impression recorded via photograph or scan, or by mould
	2. Match impression w/ object like fingerprint from police station
- **class characteristics:** general attributes of an object
	- i.e. type, make, model, style, size
	- tire's class characteristics may be
		- 12-inch
		- steel-belted radial
		- manufactured in 2005 by B.F. Goodrich Company
	- lack specific details
- **individual characteristics:** specific and unique features of object
	- i.e. specific tread wear shows its the left rear tire of Ford Taurus

#### Fingerprints

- **fingerprint:** patterned mark left on a surface by a fingertip
	- always unique to an individual (even identical twins)
	- patterns never change
- **visible fingerprint:** fingerprint observable to the naked eye
	- formed when coated in dirt, blood, grease, or some other substance
	- can be photographed immediately
- **latent fingerprint:** print formed by natural oils that form on the skin's surface, can't be seen by naked eye
	- must be developed before photographed
	- prints on non-absorbed surfaces (i.e. metal, plastic) dusted using graphite powder that sticks to ridges
		- print lifted using adhesive tape and placed on white cardboard surface before photograph
	- "iodine fuming" used to lift prints from absorbent surfaces (i.e. paper, cloth), prints absorb iodine fumes and become visible
	- laser beam used to illuminate print, print absorbs laser and turns yellow

#### Gloves

- Police use glove impressions to identify a suspect
- Compare gloves' class and individual characteristics w/ gloves of a suspect
- For criminals who try to hide their fingerprints using gloves

#### Shoe Prints and Tire Tracks

- Matched with suspect's shoes or tires
- If police can find 2 left footprints and 2 right footprints, they get general idea of suspect's
	- approx. height and weight
	- injuries he/she might have got from the crime
	- whether suspect was carrying, pushing, or dragging anything
	- whether he/she was running

### Body Elements and DNA

- Crimes against people often result in transfer of bodily fluids or other bodily elements, like...
	- blood
	- semen
	- mucus
	- sputum
	- hair
	- skin
- Blood most common body substance found at crime scene
- Blood samples send to determine whether blood is human
- Then human blood analyzed whether it matches blood type of victim / suspect
- Blood types class characteristics, DNA testing required for further narrowing
- Hair / clothing fibers can be easily transferred from offender to victim
- Police can use fibres to match those found in suspect's clothes, car, or home
- Investigations can send fibers for forensic analysis

#### DNA Testing

- **DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid):** building block of a person's genetic makeup
- Determines person's physical characteristics
	- height
	- weight
	- hair colour
- DNA *pattern* different for each person, except for identical twins
- DNA "print" can be compared w/ DNA profile from crime scene sample
	- match = samples came from same person
	- no match: suspect &ne; perpetrator

### Procedures for Labelling Evidence

- Must label items of evidence so that they are easily identified at later date
	- must maintain **chain of custody**
- **chain of custody:** witnessed, written record of all the people who maintained unbroken control over items of evidence
	- must remain unbroken and show
		- who had contact with the evidence
		- the dates and times the evidence was handled
		- the circumstances under which the evidence was handled
		- what changes, if any, were made to the evidence
- **"evidence package":** package where tagged evidence is placed &mdash; *Information on tag and package:*
	- brief description of the item
	- police case number
	- date when the evidence was collected
	- location of collection
	- brand name of the item, if any
	- serial number or clothing information
	- name and badge number of officer who collected the evidence
	- destination of the item for analysis or storage

## Arrest and Detention

### Questioning the Accused

- Police officers required to ask suspects questions as they investigate crime
- They cannot *force* a suspect to answer their questions
	- s. 7 of *Charter* grants right to remain silent &mdash; [[c4-charter-rights-freedoms]]	
- Police must give suspect a chance to "make a free and meaningful choice about whether to speak or remain silent."
	- *R*. v. *Liew*, [1999] 3 S.C.R. 227
- Police required by law to promptly inform arrested persons of the reason of their arrest and say (due to procedure):
	> You have the right to retain and instruct legal counsel without delay. You have the right to telephone any lawyer that you wish. You also have the right to free legal advice from a legal aid lawyer. If you are charged with an offence, you can contact the Legal Aid Plan for legal assistance. Do you understand? Do you wish to telephone a lawyer now?
- Anything person says to police or puts in writing can be used against him or her in court
- Young ppl. given special rights under *Youth Criminal Justice Act* &mdash; [[c12-criminal-law-young-ppl]]

#### Interrogation Techniques

- Primary goal: obtain the *truth*
- *Best way:* Develop trusting relationship with suspect
- *Beginning of questioning:* Police tend use open-ended, non-threatening questions
	- i.e. "Tell me what happened."
	- designed to encourage suspect to talk about incident
	- ...and provide answers w/ lots of info.
- Investigators later ask closed questions
	- i.e. "What time did you leave your house?"
	- designed to gather specific answers
- **4-Stage Interrogation Process**
	1. the entire incident
	2. the period before the offence took place
	3. the details of the actual offence
	4. the period following the offence

### Arrest and Detention Procedures

- *Start:* Police formally charges person w/ committing an offence
- Then, police arrests or detains suspect
- **arrest:** legally depriving someone of liberty by seizing or touching the person to indicate that he/she is in custody
	- *Conditions for a Lawful Arrest*
		1. identify himself / herself as a police officer
		2. advise the accused that he/she is under arrest
		3. inform the accused promptly of the charge and show the arrest warrant if one has been obtained
		4. touch the accused to indicated that he/she is in legal custody
		5. once accused in custody, police must inform him/her of the right to counsel
- **detention:** legally depriving a person of liberty for the purpose of asking questions, w/ or w/o physical restraint
	- people being detained must be promptly informed of reasons for detention
	- must be informed of their right to retain counsel
	- i.e. assault case
		- victim is conscious and able to give police description of person who assaulted him
		- police comb neighbourhood and find Francis, who fits description
		- they ask Francis to come to the station to answer a few questions
		- Francis doesn't have a choice on the matter
		- Francis may call a lawyer upon arrival to the station
- Person must be arrested / detained on reasonable grounds
- **reasonable grounds:** information that would lead a reasonable person to conclude that the suspect had committed a criminal offence
	- i.e. Rob is running away from a bank robbery w/ a sack of money
- Respectable citizens usually cooperate w/ police
	- When questioning persists beyond appropriate point,
	- individual may demand to speak to a lawyer and be given officer's name and badge number
	- someone can sue for unlawful arrest if detained / arrested in arbitrary or improper manner
- 3 methods of apprehending offender

#### Appearance Notice

- for most summary offences and less serious indictable offences
- **appearance notice:** legal document compelling accused to appear in court on a certain date and time
	- accused must sign and be given copy
- **bench warrant:** arrest warrant issued directly by Judge when an accused person fails to appear in court
	- accused charged w/ additional offence: "failure to appear"
	- more difficult to be released from custody before court date

#### Arrest with a Warrant

- for suspected serious indictable offences
- **summons:** legal document issued for an indictable offence, ordering accused to appear in court
	- happens when police believe suspect will appear voluntarily
	- informs accused of charges and when to appear in court
	- typically directed to go to police stn. for fingerprinting
	- failure = bench warrant
- **information:** statement given under oath, informing the Court of the details of the offence
	- when police believes suspect will *not* appear in court willingly
	- Judge decides whether to issue an **arrest warrant**
- **arrest warrant:** written court order directing police to arrest the suspect
	- provides name of the accused
	- offence person is charged with
	- reason for warrant

#### Arrest Without a Warrant

- 3 Circumstances Where Police can be Arrested without Warrant &mdash; s. 495 of *Criminal Code*
	- they have reasonable grounds to suspect a person has either committed an indictable offence or is about to commit one
	- they find person in the act of committing a criminal offence
	- they find person whom they believe is named on arrest warrant
- Provisions in s. 495 are extended to all **peace officers**
- **peace officer:** person responsible for preserving the public peace, like
	- police officer
	- mayor
	- prison guard
	- customs officer
	- aircraft pilot
	- fisheries officer

#### Citizen's Arrest

- **citizen's arrest:** arrest without a warrant by any person other than a peace officer
- above rare due to fear of being sued for false arrest or of being injured
	- typical citizen's arrest: shoplifter arrested by security guard
- s. 494 of *Criminal Code* &mdash; limitations of citizen's arrest
	1. Anyone may arrest without a warrant
		- (a) a person whom he(/she) finds committing an indictable offence or
		- (b) a person who, on reasonable grounds, he(/she) believes
			- (i) has committed a criminal offence, and
			- (ii) is escaping from and freshly pursued by persons who have lawful authority to arrest that person
	2. If an individual is committing a criminal offence on or in *relation to property*, that person may be arrested without a warrant by
		- (a) the owner or a person in lawful possession of the property, or
		- (b) a person authorized by the owner or by a person in lawful possession of the property

### Searches

Police generally need a warrant to search and Canadians are protected by s. 8 of *Charter* &mdash; [[c4-charter-rights-freedoms]]

***Exceptions below***

#### Searching a Person

- According to S.C.C. decision in *R.* v. *Stillman*, [1997] 1 S.C.R. 607
- No warrant required, as long as...
	- arrest is lawful
	- search is connected to the lawful arrest
	- manner in which the search is carried out must be reasonable
- Person does not have to supply police w/ breath, blood, or urine sample w/o warrant
	- *exception:* suspicion of impaired driving
	- even with warrant, arrested person can talk to lawyer before providing
	- police may obtain warrant to force one to provide DNA sample for "designated offences", such as:
		- murder
		- aggravated sexual assault
- Most cases: Police will do cursory search or "pat-down" immediately after arrest to ensure person not carrying weapons or concealing evidence

#### Searching a Place

- **search warrant:** court document that gives the police the right to search a specific location
- police must ensure warrant is correctly obtained and filled out, otherwise Court will ignore evidence obtained via warrant
- Steps to Obtain Search Warrant (Police)
	1. Police officer must deliver sworn information to a Judge or Justice of the Peace
	2. Information specifies
		- crime
		- items police are looking for
		- reasonable grounds they have for believing that those items will be found at that location
	3. Court decides to grant or reject warrant
	4. If Court grants, document lists all details and date / time police are allowed to conduct search
- For searching a residence, warrant specifies
	- one day during which search may be carried out
	- unless otherwise noted, search must take place during daylight hours
		- specific: 6:00 - 21:00 (9 pm)
- Before conducting search...
	- Police must identify themselves
	- Show warrant to person living or working in the place
- During search...
	- Police may confiscate other items not listed in search warrant, as long as...
		- items are related to crime
		- items are in plain view
- After search...
	- Objects used as evidence kept in police custody until trial
	- Other items must be returned to owner within 3 months
- **telewarrant:** search warrant obtained by phone or fax
	- officer gives information over phone
	- judge / justice makes record of information and files it with the Court
	- officer then makes a facsimile warrant and shows warrant at scene of search
- Exceptions to search w/o search warrant &mdash; s. 529 (3) of *Criminal Code*
	- Police must have reasonable grounds to believe that entering the dwelling is necessary to prevent...
		1. imminent injury or death to any person
		2. destruction of evidence relating to an indictable offence
- *Controlled Drugs and Substances Act*
	- gives police authority to search any premises *except* a person's residence...
	- ... for illegal drugs w/o first obtaining warrant
	- anyone found within premises can also be searched if police have reason to believe they are carrying illegal drugs
- Provincial liquor laws
	- gives police right to search motor vehicles for illegal alcohol w/o warrant
	- warrant needed to search residence

### Procedures After Arrest

- Only suspect police have right to photograph and fingerprint is someone who has been arrested for an indictable offence
- If police do not charge or if suspect acquitted, police will usually retain arrest record for 10 yrs. before destruction
	- incl. fingerprints and photographs
- **lineup:** grouping of photographs shown to a victim or a witness for the purpose of identifying the perpetrator
	- usually people in lineup are all of same general characteristics of
		- age
		- race
		- hair length
		- etc.
	- police cannot force suspect, but it may work in suspect's favour

## Pretrial Release

- Police may release accused until trial
- **promise to appear:** signed agreement that the accused will appear in court at the time of the trial
	- given if police believe accused will appear in court voluntarily and will not commit any offences while awaiting trial
	- Court may issue bench warrant for failure to appear
- **recognizance:** guarantee that the accused will appear in court when required, w/ fine up to $500 for failure
	- deposit usually not required unless...
	- accused from another province or lives >200 km away
- **surety:** person who agrees to make a payment if the accused does not appear at trial
	- also has to sign recognizance form

### Bail

- **bail:** temporary release of an accused who posts money or some other security
- Bail hearing must be held before Justice of the Peace within 24 h of arrest if possible
- s. 11(e) of *Charter* guarantees that no one is to be denied reasonable bail without just cause
- **show-cause hearing:** judicial hearing where Crown or accused has to convince the Judge either to detain or release accused before trial
	- "Cause" incl. flight (escape) risk, risk to public safety, or any other just cause
	- Crown successful = Judge issues detention order
- **reverse onus:** burden of proof shifts to defence; the defence must show why bail should be granted &mdash; *happens when...*
	- charge is murder
	- accused is charged w/ committing indictable offence while already out on bail
	- offence is indictable and accused is not a Canadian resident
	- charge involves failure to appear or breach of a bail condition
	- accused is charged with importing, trafficking, or possession for trafficking narcotics (or conspiracy for these)
	- as of May 1, 2008 &mdash; *Tackling Violent Crime Act*, charge is...
		- using firearm to commit certain serious offences:
			- attempted murder
			- discharging firearm with intent
			- sexual assault with a weapon
			- aggravated sexual assault
			- kidnapping
			- hostage-taking
			- robbery
			- extortion
		- indictable firearms-related offence where accused under firearms prohibition order
		- firearms trafficking
		- possession for the purpose of weapons trafficking
		- firearms smuggling
- Additional factors for *reverse onus*
	- whether firearm was used in offence
	- whether accused faces mandatory min. punishment of imprisonment of 3 years or more for a firearms offence

### *Habeas Corpus*

- Refer to [[c1-law-and-society]]
- **writ of *habeas corpus*:** appeal of bail refusal if bail is denied and accused believes that he/she has been illegally detained
