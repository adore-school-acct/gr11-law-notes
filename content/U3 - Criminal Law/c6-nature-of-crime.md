# C6 - The Nature of Crime

## Defining Crime and Criminal Offences

**crime:** any act or omission of an act that is prohibited and punishable by federal law

### 4 Key Elements of a Crime

1. Act considered wrong by society
2. Act causes harm to society in general or to those who need protection (i.e. minors)
3. Harm is serious
4. Remedy handled by criminal justice system

### Criminal Law

**Criminal law:** body of laws that prohibit and punish acts that injure people, property, and society as a whole

Crimes are considered acts against ***society as a whole***

*Reason:* If item is stolen from a shopkeeper, shopkeeper will have to increase prices to compensate for the losses, everyone pays for the loss

*Reason 2:* If someone assaults someone and isn't punished, another person will assault another person

#### Purpose of Criminal Law

- protect people and property
- maintain order
- preserve standards of public decency

### The *Criminal Code*

- ***Criminal Code of Canada*:** Federal statue that contains majority of criminal laws passed by Parliament
  - lists offences and sentences
  - lists procedures to follow when trying someone of these crimes
  - *Criminal Code* is amended when new threats arise or actions are no longer considered crimes
    - i.e. 2007: Bill C-19 passed, changed sentences for personal injury, terrorism, and organized crime

#### New Crimes

- Hacking
- Cyberbullying
- Piracy
- Unprotected sex
- Scamming
- Darknet markests
- Online funding of crime
- Texting and driving

#### Used to be Crimes and Remedies

Adultery, death penalty, marijuana

### History of the *Criminal Code*

- Before Conf. 1867: Each province responsible for its own criminal laws
- Canada's criminal law system (excl. Québec) inherited from Britian
- John A. Macdonald set out to create a uniform set of criminal laws
- After Confederation: Criminal offence considered offence regardless of where it was committed
- After Conf. 1867: Sentence would no longer vary from province to province
- **S. 91 (27) of the *Constitution Act, 1867***: Grants fed. gov't. power to make criminal law for all of Canada
- July 1892: *Criminal Code of Canada* (statute) passed
  - called a "code" bcz. it combined desc. of crime and criminal law procedure in single statute
- Reformations of the *Criminal Code*
  - 1953: Reduced from 1,100 to 753 sections
  - 1986: Revised draft to make code less complicated, more organized, and easier to understand
  - No full complete revision of Code since 1892
- Other criminal statutes
  - *Controlled Drugs and Substances Act*
  - *Customs Act*
  - *Competition Act*
  - *Youth Criminal Justice Act*
  - *Food and Drugs Act*
  - *Income Tax Act*

### Provincial Jurisdiction

- Provinces have power to pass laws on matters related to their jurisdiction
- **quasi-criminal laws:** laws covering less serious criminal offences at the provincial / municipal level
  - most often punishable by fines
  - in few cases, offenders are sent to jail
  - Ex. in Ont.
    - *Liquor Control Act*
    - *Highway Traffic Act*
    - *Wildlife Act*
  
## The Elements of a Crime

### Formula

$$
\begin{equation*}
\text{actus reus} + \text{mens rea} = \textbf{crime}
\end{equation*}
$$

Crown must prove both of these actions for the act to be considered a crime

### *Actus Reus*

***actus reus*:** "the guilty act", the voluntary action, omission or state of being that is forbidden under the *Criminal Code*

- **action:** doing a forbidden act
  - i.e. punching someone in the face
- **omission:** not doing an act that you are obliged to do
  - i.e. not supervising a child, leaving pitbull unleashed
- **state of being:** being in a forbidden place / state
  - i.e. not clearing driveway, leaving biz. open on prob. day
  - i.e. being in possession of stolen or break-in tools, being caught in an illegal casino

### *Mens Rea*

***mens rea*:** "the guilty mind", a deliberate intention to commit a wrongful act, w/ reckless disregard for the consequences

#### Intent

- **intent:** state of mind where person desires to carry out wrongful act, knows what the results will be, and is reckless regarding the consequences
- **general intent:** desire to commit a wrongful act w/ no ulterior motive or purpose
  - i.e. Rob punches Bob 'cuz he mad
- **specific intent:** desire to commit one wrongful act to accomplish another act
  - i.e. Rob punches Bob 'cuz he wants to steal his credit cards

#### Motive (supporting evidence, not an element)

**motive:** *reason* why a person commits a crime

- does not establish *mens rea*
- Crown must prove that act was "planned and deliberate" to show *specific intent*

#### Knowledge

**knowledge:** accused had knowledge of certain facts before / during crime, can be used for *mens rea*

- Examples
  - s. 368 (1)(a) of *Criminal Code*: "Every one who, knowing that a document is forged, uses, deals, or acts upon it," guilty of an offence of circulating a forged document
  - Rob's friend Rock is shown a plan to beat up Bob, but Rock does nothing about it

#### Criminal Negligence

**criminal negligence:** wanton or reckless disregard for the lives and safety of others, sometimes causing serious injury or death

Definiton of *criminal neglience* under s. 219 (1) of the *Criminal Code*:

> Everyone is criminally negligent who
>
> - (a) in doing anything, or
> - (b) in omitting to do anything that it is his duty to do
>
> shows wanton or reckless disregard for the lives and safety of others

Example: Bob leaves his gun on a table, his 4-year-old child Bobby takes the gun and accidentally shoots his friend, Foffy

#### Recklessness

**recklessness:** conciously taking an unjustifiable risk that a reasonable person would not take

- Examples
  - Drag racing on the street and causing a big accident
  - Driving while not wearing your prescription glasses, if you need them

#### Willful Blindness

**willful blindness:** deliberately closing of one's mind to the possible consequences of their actions

- Example
  - a monitor with a school board engraving is being sold to you, but you still buy it, ignoring the fact that it is most likely stolen
  - a drug dealer visits your house and you don't search the house to see if they hid drugs in your place

### Regulatory Laws

- **regulatory laws:** federal or provincial statues meant to protect public welfare
  - *Mens rea* not required
  - Legislators must write *willfully* or *with intent* for the law to require *mens rea*
- **strict liability offences:** offences that do not require *mens rea*, but accused can offer defense of *due diligence*
  - **due diligence:** defence that accused took every reasonable precaution to avoid committing a particular offence
  - i.e. of due diligence: Person slips and falls in a mall. Mall owner says they took every precaution by cleaning the mall hourly and posting a schedule, placing wet floor signs, and designing the mall in a way to reduce trip and slip hazards (i.e. grooves in pavement)
- **absolute liability offence:** offences that do not require *mens rea* and accused can offer no defence
  - person cannot be imprisoned for these offences
  - usual penalty: *fine*

## Involvement in a Crime

### Perpetrator

**perpetrator:** person who actually commits crime

**co-perpetrator:** perpetrator when they commit a crime w/ multiple perpretrators

i.e. Rob the Bank Robber points a gun in the bank. He is a *perpetrator*

### Aiding

**parties to an offence:** people who are indirectly involved in committing a crime

**aiding:** criminal offence that involves helping a perpetrator commit a crime

i.e. Ron the bankteller shows Rob the robber a way to enter the bank where there is less security. Ron is *aiding* Rob and is a *party to the offence*.

### Abetting

**abetting:** crime of encouraging the perpetrator to commit an offence

i.e. Hardy cheers and says "Hit harder!" when Rock beats up Billy on the streets. Hardy is *abetting* the crime.

### Counselling

**counselling:** crime of advising, recommending, or persuading another person to commit a crime

i.e. Ronnie the bank manager tells his friend Ron on ways to evade paying his taxes. Ronnie is guilty of *counselling* Ron.

### Accessory After the Fact

**accessory after the fact:** someone who knowingly receives, comforts, or assists a perpetrator escaping from the police

i.e. Rock offers a place for Rob to hide from the cops after he robbed a bank. Rock is aware that Rob robbed a bank. Rock is an *accessory after the fact*.

### Party to Common Intention

**party to common intention:** shared responsibility among criminals for any additional offences that are commited in the process of the crime they originally intended to commit

i.e. While Rob robs a bank, his buddy Robulous decides to vandalize the bank.

Rob will be guilty of the original offence since he knew that would've been a likely consequence.

Rob and Robulous have a *party to common intention*.

#### Exception for Murder

Second offence is murder or attempted murder

Participant in o.g. crime will only be responsible if he had **actual** knowledge that the murder was likely to happen

### Incomplete Crimes

#### Attempt

**attempt:** the intention to commit a crime, even when the crime is not completed

- Accused had intent to commit the crime, but failed to carry it through
- Requires *actus reus*: Begins when mere preparation turns into action required to commit an offence
  - i.e. Rock fires his gun at Billy trying to run away from him, but Rock misses and runs out of bullets
  - i.e. Bombers plan to bomb a school and construct a bomb, but they are caught by the police before they execute their plan.

#### Conspiracy

**conspiracy:** agreement between 2+ ppl. to commit an illegal act, even if act does not occur

- Even if conspirators change their minds or don't get a chance to carry out act, they are still guilty bcz. they agreed to it once
- i.e. Waluigi and Wario plan to murder Mario, but the hitman they hired was an undercover cop. Waluigi and Wario are charged with *conspiracy* for planning to kill Mario
